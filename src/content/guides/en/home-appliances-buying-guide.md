The two scariest things about buying appliances are getting lost in a wall of specs and being swayed by unboxing-day excitement. This guide does not dump specs — it gives you **a buying logic for each high-frequency category**: what to look at first, second, and who to consult.

All creators mentioned have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and verified data.

## Universal three steps: need first, model second

Before anything else, answer three questions:

1. **Budget and space**: how much money, where it goes, the size ceiling.
2. **Core need**: what you care about most — power saving, quiet, capacity, or looks? Rank priorities; you cannot have all.
3. **Check real reviews**: take the first two answers and search long-term category reviews, not unboxing-day impressions.

The categories below build on this.

## TV: size first, picture quality by principle and data

**Size first**: set by viewing distance — close means smaller, far means larger. Set size before anything else.

**Then the three picture-quality factors**: panel type, dimming zones, chip tuning.

- **[Geekerwan](/en/accounts/geekerwan)**: explains the underlying logic of panel, dimming, and chip — builds your picture-quality standard.
- **[Xiaobai Pingce](/en/accounts/xiaobai-pingce)**: same-room round-ups give comparable objective data for choosing between candidates.

**Action**: after size is set, use Geekerwan to understand "why good" and Xiaobai to compare "who better", then order.

## Fridge: capacity and cooling before brand

**Capacity first**: set by household size and shopping habits (a three-person home and a three-generation home differ hugely).

**Then cooling method**: fan, direct, or hybrid each has trade-offs; energy rating and the core component (compressor) decide long-term running cost.

- **[Mr Mideng](/en/accounts/mr-mideng)**: hardcore teardowns and data — good for the specific model.
- **[Zhidian Xiaochun](/en/accounts/zhidian-xiaochunge)**: buying guides set direction first.

**Action**: watch Zhidian Xiaochun to build category awareness, then Mr Mideng's round-ups to compare models.

## Washer: type and motor are the divide

**Type first**: front-load or top-load, set by space, habit, and garment type.

**Then motor and core metrics**: motor type, wash ratio, noise matter more than flashy features.

- Same references: **[Zhidian Xiaochun](/en/accounts/zhidian-xiaochunge)** and **[Mr Mideng](/en/accounts/mr-mideng)** for teardowns and comparisons.

## AC: tonnage and rating must match the area

**Tonnage first**: set by room area and orientation — too small fails to cool, too big wastes.

**Then rating and installation**: energy grade affects long-term electricity; installation quality (copper pipe, vacuuming) often matters more than the machine itself.

- **[Mr Mideng](/en/accounts/mr-mideng)**'s big-appliance logic applies to AC selection too.

**Action**: set tonnage by area, then compare rating and installation reputation among candidates.

## Cleaning gadgets: long-term real use is gold

Robot vacuums and floor washers are exactly where unboxing impressions skew optimistic; real experience lives in "one month later" content.

- **[Da Li Zi](/en/accounts/da-li-zi)**: grounded, long-term retrospectives closest to a real home.

**Action**: search only for reviews tagged "long-term / one month later"; skip pure unboxing.

## Small appliances: breadth reviews first

Air fryers, blenders, rice cookers — many categories, many models. Scan breadth reviews to build a baseline, then lock a model.

- **[Jiadian Jiupingce](/en/accounts/jiadian-jiu-pingce)**: broad coverage, good for scanning.
- **[Aifou](/en/accounts/aifou-keji)**: distinct point of view, good for cross-checking.

## Materials and safety: leave it to the lab

Coatings, materials, and hazardous substances are rarely volunteered by marketing.

- **[Laoba Pingce](/en/accounts/laoba-pingce)**: lab-based safety reviews — your "safety floor checklist".

## Macro wind: round-ups for the big picture

When you have not locked a category and want to see what to buy overall:

- **[Huangjia Pingce](/en/accounts/huangjia-pingce)**: cross-category comprehensive reviews.
- **[Gude Rebang](/en/accounts/gude-rebang)**: tech hot lists and industry observation — what is a scam right now.

## Compress buying into three lines

1. **Need before model** — answer budget, space, core need, then search reviews.
2. **Cross-check, watch long-term** — at least two channels with different stances; prefer "one month later" content.
3. **Safety to the lab, picture quality to principle** — materials to the testing lab, specs to those who explain the principle.
