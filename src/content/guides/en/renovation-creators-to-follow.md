Renovation is one of the few purchases where an ordinary household spends a large sum on a long, largely irreversible decision chain. Information asymmetry is severe, and the quality gap between renovation creators is enormous — some genuinely take you onto the site and open up a wall, others simply film a show home beautifully.

This guide does not rank by follower count. It groups creators by **the stage of the project you are actually in**, because consuming the wrong content at the wrong stage is the most common way beginners waste time.

All seven creators below have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and any verified public figures.

## Stage 1 · Before work starts: build materials literacy

The goal here is not choosing a style. It is working out what is genuinely good and what is a premium you are paying for nothing.

### [Mr Mideng](/en/accounts/mr-mideng)

A home and renovation explainer on Bilibili known for hardcore reviews. His work covers teardowns of building materials, side-by-side appliance data, and industry practices that suppliers would rather not discuss. He has been selected for Bilibili's annual top-100 creator list for several consecutive years.

**Who it suits**: budget-conscious owners worried about being talked into the wrong products.
**How to use it**: before committing to a category — flooring, tiles, ducted air conditioning — watch his coverage of that category first, and write down the specifications he keeps returning to rather than the brand names.

### [Laoba Pingce](/en/accounts/laoba-pingce)

A Hangzhou-based consumer-safety testing organisation that runs its own laboratory. Coverage spans appliances and household goods, published across Douyin, Bilibili, Xiaohongshu, and WeChat.

**Who it suits**: families with young children, or anyone particularly concerned about formaldehyde and material safety.
**How to use it**: treat it as a safety floor checklist rather than a shopping list. Its value is telling you what to rule out.

## Stage 2 · During construction: site practice and acceptance

Once the design is signed off, build quality is what actually determines the outcome. What most owners lack at this point is knowing which milestone to inspect and what to look for.

### [Zhufaner Laoyang](/en/accounts/zhufaner-laoyang)

Principal of Zhufaner, a renovation service operating in Beijing and Shanghai. His Bilibili channel covers plumbing and electrical acceptance, common material misconceptions, how to choose built-in joinery, and final handover inspection.

**Who it suits**: owners who have already signed a contract and are about to start or are mid-build.
**How to use it**: match videos to your construction milestones, watch before each site visit, and turn his checks into a physical checklist.

One caveat worth stating plainly: he runs a renovation business. That means the experience comes from real projects, but it also means that on questions like "should I use a firm or an independent crew" his position is naturally inclined. Take the experience, reach your own conclusion.

## Stage 3 · Spatial design: layout redesign on a budget

### [Designer Xiaoju](/en/accounts/shejishi-xiaoju)

Focused on small and awkwardly shaped apartments, with a large body of before-and-after cases on older walk-up flats and warm timber interiors, concentrating on spatial efficiency, storage, and cost-effectiveness.

**Who it suits**: younger urban owners on tight budgets, especially those with older, small, or irregular floor plans.
**How to use it**: find a case close to your own area and structure, and study how she handles the three hard constraints — load-bearing walls, daylight, and storage.

### [Wuli Shejijie](/en/accounts/wuli-shejijie)

Publishes approachable, bite-sized renovation know-how: choosing tiles, sliding doors and curtains, socket placement mistakes, and small-apartment makeovers, aimed at first-time renovators.

**Who it suits**: complete beginners renovating for the first time.
**How to use it**: treat it as a dictionary. When you hit a term you do not know or a small decision you cannot make — socket positions, curtain tracks, door types — search her coverage of it.

## Stage 4 · Whole-project view and taste

### [Designer Ashuang](/en/accounts/shejishi-ashuang)

A leading home and lifestyle creator on Douyin who makes dry renovation expertise genuinely easy to follow, covering pitfalls, good home design, and buying guides.

**Who it suits**: people who prefer short video and want a fast overview of the whole process.
**How to use it**: good for building a mental map of what the stages even are; return to the deeper channels above for specific decisions.

### [Xiaoai Dashu](/en/accounts/xiaoai-dashu)

A leading property-focused creator on Bilibili, known for touring high-end homes. Drawing on nearly two decades in real estate, he unpacks architecture, materials, and ways of living in accessible terms.

**Who it suits**: anyone wanting to sharpen their spatial judgement and understand what makes a home well designed.
**How to use it**: do not treat it as a template — luxury solutions rarely transfer to ordinary apartments. Watch it to calibrate your sense of proportion, circulation, and material quality.

## How to judge whether a renovation creator is trustworthy

This may be more useful than the seven names. Three workable tests:

**Does the channel show real construction?** Accounts with only renderings and finished shots have limited value. What a wall looks like once opened, how services are routed, what fails at inspection — that footage is the irreplaceable part.

**Are commercial relationships disclosed?** Commercial partnerships are extremely dense in this category. A creator who labels an ad as an ad is more credible than one who never mentions it. Note too: a creator who never names a downside of any brand usually is not describing a world without downsides.

**Is the reasoning checkable?** "This brand is good" is worth nothing. "Look for this specification above this threshold, because it determines X" is worth a lot. The first asks for your trust; the second gives you the ability to judge for yourself.

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a style before setting a budget.** The order is backwards. Style is the last, most superficial layer; structure, services, and materials are where the money goes.
- **Copying a creator's solution wholesale.** Floor plans, orientation, ceiling height, local climate, and labour costs all differ. What transfers is the method of judgement, not the specific solution.
- **Trusting a single source.** A testing lab, a contractor, and a designer routinely reach different conclusions about the same question. Only cross-checking reveals the full picture.
