Silver-hair creators are among the most moving parts of content platforms lately. They do not lean on filters or personas — they lean on decades of craft, experience, and composure.

This guide does not rank by follower count. It groups creators by **the content theme you want**.

All ten creators below have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and any verified public figures.

## Traditional craft: silent craftsmanship

### [Grandpa Amu](/en/accounts/amu-yeye)

A rural carpenter whose wordless videos of mortise-and-tenon furniture — built without nails or glue — went viral at home and abroad, dubbed "the modern Lu Ban". Most of his videos are silent; the craft speaks.

**Who it suits**: lovers of traditional craft who are healed by "slow work, fine results".
**How to use it**: as decompression and aesthetics, and good to watch with kids to quietly teach what craftsmanship means.

## Elegant aging: age is irrelevant

### [North Sea Grandpa](/en/accounts/beihai-yeye)

A dapper, ritual-filled old gentleman who cared for his ailing wife for 21 years and now moves millions with a refined, composed retirement — the byword for "aging with grace".

**Who it suits**: anyone wanting to see "you can still be sharp when old" — especially older men and their families.
**How to use it**: learn the attitude — ritual costs care, not money.

### [Fashion Grannies](/en/accounts/shishang-nainaituan)

A troupe of retirees averaging 65 who went viral with a cheongsam catwalk, spreading "Chinese elders can live beautifully" through runways and live commerce.

**Who it suits**: women wanting to break the "old means plain" stereotype.
**How to use it**: as aesthetic and mindset reference — see how they turn age into an advantage.

## Health: essential science

### [BTV Health Hall](/en/accounts/btv-yangshengtang)

A decade-plus national health show whose Douyin account condenses expert TCM, nutrition, and disease-prevention talk into short clips — a daily health stop for the aging audience.

**Who it suits**: families watching parents' and their own health, especially silver-hair viewers.
**How to use it**: build baseline health awareness and learn which signals mean seeing a doctor; for specific disease and medication, follow proper medical opinion — not a substitute for diagnosis.

## Ordinary seniors' grace: being seen

### [Han Na Bai Chuan](/en/accounts/hannabaiji)

A 66-year-old retiree who taught himself editing to film cooking and park talent; his "harmonica grandpa" video passed 100 million views, hoping to "show the grace of silver-hair elders to the public".

**Who it suits**: anyone who wants to see ordinary later life can be wonderful.
**How to use it**: as a "senior grace album"; also inspiration for those documenting their own elders.

### [Mona's Uncle](/en/accounts/monadashushu)

Films his refined daily life with father North Sea Grandpa; the father-son duo spreads "age-irrelevant" ritual through suits, piano, and world travel.

**Who it suits**: those focused on intergenerational companionship and how to get along with parents.
**How to use it**: watch both the grandfather's state and the son's ritualised companionship — enlightening for adult children.

## Master cooking: real craft

### [Lao Fan Gu](/en/accounts/laofangu)

A group of retired state-banquet masters who, on Bilibili, demystify classic dishes with zero secrets held back, bringing top culinary craft into ordinary homes.

**Who it suits**: those wanting to seriously improve and learn technical dishes.
**How to use it**: as a "master class" — learn technique and heat logic, not just the recipe.

## Rural warmth: hearth and homesickness

### [Grandma Pan](/en/accounts/panlaolao)

A rural grandma from Anhui whose earth-stove cooking and village life hit millions' homesickness and also drive local produce out of the mountains.

**Who it suits**: those working away from home missing hometown flavour, and anyone wanting real rural life.
**How to use it**: as "digital hometown food" with meals; also a real sample of rural revival.

### [Qinba Grandma](/en/accounts/qinba-nainai)

A rural couple in Shaanxi filmed by their grandson; plain, warm kitchen videos let viewers dream of home taste.

**Who it suits**: audience overlapping with Grandma Pan but preferring southern-Shaanxi flavour and grandparent warmth.
**How to use it**: pair with Grandma Pan to feel different regions' rural hearth.

## Culture: bringing characters alive

### [Uncle Hanzi](/en/accounts/hanzi-shushu)

American sinologist Richard Sears, in his 70s, drawing and explaining on Bilibili how Chinese characters evolved from oracle bone to today, bridging cultures with love.

**Who it suits**: those interested in character culture and etymology; also good for teaching kids to read.
**How to use it**: as a culture primer — each episode understands one character's "past and present", far more fun than rote memorisation.

## How to judge whether a silver-hair creator is worth following long-term

Three workable tests:

**Sincerity, not pity-selling.** Good silver-hair content shows vitality, not milking age for sympathy.

**Transfers life wisdom you can take away.** Whether cooking, health, or attitude, content you can carry off is good content.

**Respects the audience.** Treats seniors as mature viewers, not easy marketing targets.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating Health Hall as a prescription.** Popular science is a cognition tool, not a diagnosis. See a doctor for specific conditions.
- **Watching by follower count only.** Many moving silver-hair accounts are not the biggest, but more real.
- **Treating 'old' as the selling point.** Those that last have content valuable in itself; age is just the backdrop.
