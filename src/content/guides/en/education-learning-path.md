Many people want to "learn something" via content platforms, yet after half a year remember only a few memes. The problem is not effort — it is **not building a content-source portfolio**. Watching a different influencer each day never builds a system.

This guide gives a reusable method: self-study in four steps, each mapped to "who to watch, how to use it".

## Step 1 · Set the subject and level

Do not search "XX for beginners" right away. Answer two questions first:

- **Which slice?** Not the big word "learn physics" but "patch my mechanics basics" or "get what quantum even is".
- **What is my level now?** Total beginner, high-school level, or deepening? With the level set, source choices will not bounce between too hard and too easy.

**Reference**: [Li Yongle](/en/accounts/liyongle-laoshi)'s general content is best for "locating what you lack" — his breadth quickly reveals blind spots.

## Step 2 · Combine two source types

Key insight: **systematic and fun have different jobs and must be paired.**

### Systematic (the rigorous trunk)

- **[CAS Physics](/en/accounts/cas-physics)**: an authoritative institute channel giving accurate, framed knowledge.
- **[Li Yongle](/en/accounts/liyongle-laoshi)**: explains underlying principles and connects disciplines.
- **[Wu Nailao](/en/accounts/wuqeixian-wulaolao)**: life-metaphor explanations sit between systematic and fun.

**Use**: build the trunk. Establish what the knowledge looks like, then fill it in.

### Fun (hooks interest)

- **[Bidaothu](/en/accounts/bidao-thu)**, **[Xueding](/en/accounts/xueding-elema)**: science brain-teasers, light and humorous.
- **[Wuqiongxiaoliang](/en/accounts/wuqiongxiaoliang)**: biology debunking, both fun and rigorous.

**Use**: when you cannot focus, to keep curiosity alive — but never let fun replace the system.

## Step 3 · Order shallow-to-deep

Recommended order: **fun entry → systematic trunk → deep extension**.

- **Fun entry**: Bidaothu, Xueding, Wuqiongxiaoliang spark interest and build initial intuition.
- **Systematic trunk**: return to CAS Physics and Li Yongle for an accurate framework.
- **Deep extension**: drill deeper once the frame exists. For history, build a story thread with [Xiaoyuehan Kehan](/en/accounts/xiaoyuehan-kehan), then add rigour with [Lishidiaoyanshi](/en/accounts/lishidiaoyanshi).

Math runs the other way: build intuition first with [3Blue1Brown](/en/accounts/3blue1brown)'s animation (even in English), then return to a textbook for derivation. Its value is in seeing math.

Law: [Luoxiang](/en/accounts/luoxiang-shuofaxing) is both fun entry (good cases) and thinking training (logic) — can serve as the trunk directly.

## Step 4 · Test with output (Feynman technique)

Watching is not learning. The only test: **can you explain it clearly.**

**Action**: after each concept, explain it to someone who knows nothing, or write your own notes. Where you stumble is where you do not truly get it.

- Math: derive along with 3Blue1Brown's animation — more useful than rewatching.
- Science: restate a phenomenon's principle in your own words.
- History: close the video and tell the event thread yourself.

## How to judge whether a content source is reliable

Three workable tests:

**Does it explain premises and logic?** Conclusion alone is worthless; "under what premise, through what mechanism" is what has value.

**Does it separate opinion from fact?** Channels that mark "mainstream view" versus "my speculation" are more trustworthy.

**Does it cite sources?** Data and studies that trace to an origin survive checking.

## Three common mistakes

- **Chasing influencers without a system.** One fun creator cannot replace framed knowledge. Fun makes you want to learn; systematic makes you learn it right.
- **Watching without doing.** Self-study is muscle memory; restating and deriving are what internalise it.
- **Ignoring level jumps.** Raw beginners gnawing deep content get frustrated; with a base, watching entry content is waste. Order matters.
