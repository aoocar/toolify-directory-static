The biggest back-to-school trap is not buying the wrong gear — it is **being swayed by marketing into buying things you will never use**. This guide does not dump specs; it gives an incoming student an executable buying logic: what to look at first, second, and who to consult.

All creators mentioned have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and verified data.

## Step 1: How to pick your freshman laptop (three steps)

### 1. Set the need by your major

Different majors need very different laptops:

- **Liberal arts / business / light office**: documents, slides, and browsing — an integrated-GPU thin-and-light is enough.
- **STEM (programming / modeling / CAD)**: stronger CPU, discrete GPU, and more RAM — prioritize performance.
- **Design / film / animation**: the highest demands on screen color, GPU, and cooling — budget accordingly.

**Reference**: [Yingjian Chatan](/en/accounts/yingjian-chatan) explains what CPUs and GPUs actually do with animation, so you understand the hardware before you pick — hard to be fooled by marketing jargon afterward.

### 2. Set a budget band

After defining the need, draw a budget line and choose within it, rather than being led by "just add a little more and you can get XX".

**Reference**: [Zhongzheng Pingce](/en/accounts/zhongzheng-pingce) is a self-funded, ad-free reviewer focused on value-for-money — ideal for students looking for the best pick within a budget.

### 3. Check portability

At the same price point, prioritize weight, battery life, and screen size. A student carries the laptop across campus every day — **weight and battery affect daily life more than benchmark numbers**.

**Reference**: [Bibar Pingceshi](/en/accounts/bibarpingceshi) is known for measured data, chassis comparisons, and cooling tests — one of the most reliable starting points for freshman laptop choices.

## Step 2: How to choose your freshman laptop bag

The laptop bag (or sleeve) does not need to be expensive — **fit matters more than brand**. Check three things:

1. **Protection**: padded, some splash resistance, buffering daily bumps.
2. **Capacity**: can it hold the power adapter, a mouse, and textbooks — not just barely squeeze the laptop in?
3. **Commute**: if you carry it daily, prefer a lightweight backpack; for short dorm-to-classroom moves, a sleeve inside a normal bag is enough.

**Reference**: [Xihuan Jiu Mai](/en/accounts/xihuan-jiu-mai) follows a "buy rationally, buy what you need" principle and does scenario-based recommendations for laptop peripherals and bags — it helps freshmen decide whether something is actually worth buying.

## Step 3: The standard freshman kit

The "standard freshman kit" usually means **laptop + phone + earbuds**. The rule: **fill real scenarios, do not chase top specs.**

- **Laptop**: the big share of the budget — follow Step 1.
- **Phone**: good enough is good enough; do not pay for performance surplus.
- **Earbuds**: noise-cancelling for dorm study, portable for commuting — pick by usage.

**Reference**: [Geekerwan](/en/accounts/geekerwan) explains the principles behind devices, and [Xiaobai Pingce](/en/accounts/xiaobai-pingce) provides same-room comparison data. Together they tell you whether the extra money is worth it.

## Step 4: Dorm goods — buy only the high-frequency

Dorm goods are where impulse buying happens. **Only buy what you will use in the first week**: power strips (dorm outlets are usually scarce), bedding, storage, and a desk lamp. Add the rest after you move in.

**Reference**: [GuDe Hot List](/en/accounts/gude-rebang) covers student-grade gear with ad-free reviews, and [He Tongxue](/en/accounts/he-tongxue) often combines creative tech with campus life — good for inspiration.

## Step 5: After arrival — actually use your gear

Gear is only the first step; learning to use it well matters more:

- **[oeasy](/en/accounts/oeasy)**: free, systematic Office and computer tutorials from zero — build the foundation of your laptop.
- **[GenJi Is Really Trying to Teach You](/en/accounts/genji-shizhen-ai)**: AI application tutorials that turn your laptop into a learning and creation tool.

## The kit in three sentences

1. **Need first, model second** — answer major, budget, and portability before you order.
2. **Bag by protection and capacity, not price** — fit beats brand.
3. **Kit by scenario, dorm goods by frequency** — paying for "just in case" is the biggest waste.
