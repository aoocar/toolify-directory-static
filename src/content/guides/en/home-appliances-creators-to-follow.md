Home appliances are high-ticket, spec-heavy, and among the easiest things to regret after buying. The same model gets called a miracle by one person and a premium-for-nothing by another — the gap is often not the product but that you watched the wrong person.

This guide does not rank by follower count. It groups creators by **the category you are about to buy**. Decide what you want first, then find the right person.

All ten creators below have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and any verified public figures.

## Big appliances: fridge / washer / AC / TV

### [Mr Mideng](/en/accounts/mr-mideng)

A home and renovation explainer on Bilibili known for hardcore reviews. Big appliances — fridges, washers, air conditioners, TVs — with data comparisons and industry practices are his strength; selected for Bilibili's annual top-100 list for several years.

**Who it suits**: anyone buying high-ticket big appliances and wanting real teardowns and data.
**How to use it**: after picking a category, search his round-up for it; note the specs he keeps stressing (compressor, energy rating, dimming) rather than just the brand.

### [Zhidian Xiaochun](/en/accounts/zhidian-xiaochunge)

A practical buying-guide creator focused on appliances, especially strong on "how to choose" for fridges, washers, and TVs.

**Who it suits**: people who want category awareness before deciding which unit.
**How to use it**: before buying, watch his selection-logic series to answer "how much capacity, what energy rating" first, then go to Mr Mideng for model comparisons.

## TV / monitor / digital: objective data

### [Geekerwan](/en/accounts/geekerwan)

A leading Bilibili tech-deep-dive creator who explains chips, screens, and display principles thoroughly; his picture-quality logic for TVs and displays is especially hardcore.

**Who it suits**: advanced users unsatisfied with "which is good" who want "why it is good".
**How to use it**: treat his TV/display principle content as a textbook for building bottom-line picture-quality standards (panel, dimming zones, chip tuning).

### [Xiaobai Pingce](/en/accounts/xiaobai-pingce)

Known for lab-style same-room round-ups, giving comparable objective data for phones, TVs, monitors, and other digital devices.

**Who it suits**: people choosing between two or three candidate models and wanting data.
**How to use it**: once you have two or three candidates, search his same-room test and use real benchmarks for the final call.

## Kitchen and cleaning gadgets

### [Da Li Zi](/en/accounts/da-li-zi)

A leading Bilibili lifestyle creator covering real-use experiences of kitchen and cleaning gadgets (robot vacuums, floor washers), grounded and relatable.

**Who it suits**: people buying kitchen or cleaning appliances who want real household feedback.
**How to use it**: focus on her "after long use" retrospectives — closer to reality than unboxing-day impressions.

### [Jiadian Jiupingce](/en/accounts/jiadian-jiu-pingce)

A review channel covering appliances and home categories broadly — good for wide scanning.

### [Aifou](/en/accounts/aifou-keji)

With a digital-review lab background, covering phones to appliances with a distinct point of view.

**How to use these two**: as breadth supplements — when you want a quick sense of the pitfalls in an unfamiliar category, scan them first to build a baseline judgement.

## Materials and safety: testing

### [Laoba Pingce](/en/accounts/laoba-pingce)

A consumer-safety testing organisation running its own lab; appliance-related materials, safety, and hazardous substances are its core lens.

**Who it suits**: families with children, or anyone especially sensitive to material safety.
**How to use it**: treat it as a safety floor checklist — watch appliance materials, coatings, and hazardous substances that marketing tends to skip.

## Cross-category round-ups: the big picture

### [Huangjia Pingce](/en/accounts/huangjia-pingce)

A consumer-electronics review channel spanning multiple appliance categories — good for cross-category understanding.

### [Gude Rebang](/en/accounts/gude-rebang)

Known for tech hot lists and industry observation — good for the macro rhythm of "what is worth buying now, what is a scam".

**How to use these two**: when you have not locked a category and want the overall wind direction, use them for the impression, then go deep with the vertical channels above.

## How to judge whether an appliance-review channel is trustworthy

Three workable tests:

**Does it produce measured data?** "Great experience" alone is worthless; test environment, samples, and comparison data are what make it reference-worthy.

**Does it disclose sample sourcing and commercial ties?** Sent, self-purchased, or sponsored — the three sources carry naturally different credibility. Channels that state the source and label ads are more trustworthy.

**Does it explain the use case?** A good review says who a unit suits and who it does not, not just a ranking. A "number one" divorced from context is not necessarily number one for you.

## Common mistakes

- **Trusting one channel's ranking.** Different channels often contradict on the same category; cross-checking gets closer to the truth.
- **Swayed by unboxing-day impressions.** Freshness at unboxing invites praise; long-term retrospectives are objective. For gadgets especially, watch the "one month later" content.
- **Ignoring the use case.** The creator's household, family size, and floor plan differ from yours. What transfers is the method of judgement, not the direct conclusion.
