Most people learn Office the wrong way: they save a pile of "magic tricks" but fall back on old habits when it matters. The problem is not effort — it is **the order**. Chasing flashy tricks before the foundation is laid just makes the pile messier.

This guide gives a path that does not loop back on itself. Each step maps to "who to watch, what to do".

## Step 1 · Build the base: understand the software before the tricks

The goal is not one function but understanding what the software is actually doing for you.

### Watch: [oeasy](/en/accounts/oeasy)

His series starts from the interface, file structure, and object model, connecting the logic of the Office trio in one pass. Free, complete, nothing held back.

**Action**: over two weeks, go through words, sheets, and presentations in series order. You need not memorise every button — only build a map of "what this software can do and how data flows".

With the base set, every later trick has something to hang on.

## Step 2 · Break through by tool: follow real pain

Do not spread effort evenly. Open last month's work files and see which part drains the most time — attack that first.

### If you present often

- **[Pangmen PPT](/en/accounts/pangmen-ppt)**: layout principles and taste — fixes "ugly and unclear".
- **[Qiuye PPT](/en/accounts/qiuye-ppt)**: template thinking and quick wins — fixes "I must submit now".

**Action**: finish one of Pangmen's "layout principle" series to build judgement, then use Qiuye's patch tricks for emergencies. Long term, taste beats templates.

### If you live in spreadsheets

- **[Excel Caozuo](/en/accounts/excel-caozuo)**: functions, pivot tables, high-frequency operations hands-on.
- **[WPS Dalaoban](/en/accounts/wps-dalaoban)**: local WPS features and collaboration.

**Action**: list the sheets you repeat weekly (attendance, reimbursement, inventory) and rebuild each with what you learned. Real tasks are the best practice field.

## Step 3 · Workplace lookup: treat bite-sized accounts as a toolbox

With the base and a main direction, what remains is daily inspiration and quick lookup.

- **[Chenge Bangong](/en/accounts/chenge-bangong)**, **[Houge Bangong](/en/accounts/houge-bangong)**: general workplace tips with clear scenarios.
- **[Duidui Bangong](/en/accounts/duidui-bangong)**: a perspective on changing how you work, often dismantling inefficient habits.
- **[Bingbing jie](/en/accounts/bingbing-jie)**: multi-tool efficiency and communication.
- **[Sanmiao Office](/en/accounts/sanmiao-office)**: ultra-short snapshots, learnable while waiting for an elevator.

**Action**: brush during commutes; when you see a useful move, do one thing — **save it to a "to-practise" folder**, then drill two or three for real over the weekend.

## Two threads that run through everything

### Drive practice with real tasks (project-based learning)

Do not "learn then practise". The moment you learn a move, apply it to a real task on your desk. Learn pivot tables — rebuild this month's report with them. Learn masters — rebuild the department weekly template with them. Real tasks expose the gaps you thought you had closed.

### Build a personal template library

Save everything reusable you produce (weekly report, reimbursement sheet, presentation master) into one clearly named folder. Three months later that is your real asset — others still swap templates on the fly; you pull up your own.

## Three common mistakes

- **Collecting without practising.** Watching is input; doing is internalising. Without output, the folder is digital trash.
- **Chasing new plugins blindly.** Mastering pivots, masters, and basic functions before reaching for efficiency plugins is the right order; reverse it and you invert means and ends.
- **Spreading effort evenly.** Your pain sets priority. Attack the most time-draining part first for the biggest return.
