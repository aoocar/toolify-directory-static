The knowledge zone is the most worthwhile yet the easiest to be led astray by pseudo-science. This guide does not rank by follower count. It groups creators by **subject**, so you can fill the exact gap in your own knowledge.

All ten creators below have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and any verified public figures.

## Law and society

### [Luoxiang](/en/accounts/luoxiang-shuofaxing)

A professor at China University of Political Science and Law who explains criminal law and legal reasoning through vivid cases — serious law made both fun and deeply thoughtful.

**Who it suits**: ordinary readers wanting basic legal literacy and "why this verdict".
**How to use it**: easy to binge as stories, but the point is his logic-over-emotion way of analysing problems.

## General science

### [Li Yongle](/en/accounts/liyongle-laoshi)

A former physics teacher known for "explaining the deepest principle in the plainest words" — physics, math, economics, social phenomena, everything. The entry-level creator for general science.

**Who it suits**: anyone wanting a cross-disciplinary general perspective and the principles behind everyday phenomena.
**How to use it**: treat it as a general dictionary. When you hit an unexplained phenomenon (why leap years, what is quantum), search his coverage first.

### [CAS Physics](/en/accounts/cas-physics)

The official science account of the Institute of Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences — authoritative and accurate, a reliable source of serious physics.

**Who it suits**: people wanting a rigorous, unadulterated physics framework.
**How to use it**: build the authoritative trunk, then use the two below for flavour.

### [Wu Nailao](/en/accounts/wuqeixian-wulaolao)

A physics professor at Tongji University who makes physics fun with life experiments and metaphors, under the banner "understand physics without drilling problems".

**Who it suits**: people afraid of or bored by physics — especially students and parents.
**How to use it**: to break the "physics is hard" mental block, connecting abstract concepts to life.

### [Bidaothu](/en/accounts/bidao-thu)

A Tsinghua-backed fun-science creator who turns brain-teaser questions into proper experiments (how to peel crayfish by scientific method) — light and humorous.

**Who it suits**: people wanting an easy on-ramp and curiosity piqued by fun.
**How to use it**: as a science condiment to keep curiosity alive, then return to serious content for depth.

### [Xueding](/en/accounts/xueding-elema)

A science creator covering chemistry, physics, and more, often starting from daily phenomena with playful explanation.

**Who it suits**: people who like discovering science in everyday trivia.
**How to use it**: like Bidaothu, as a fun entry; cross-check with authoritative sources when it matters.

## Nature and biology

### [Wuqiongxiaoliang](/en/accounts/wuqiongxiaoliang)

Known for the "network hot-biology identification" series, correcting biology rumours with humour and rigour.

**Who it suits**: anyone interested in nature, plants and animals, and debunking.
**How to use it**: his identification series is the best rumour filter — when you see an absurd creature claim, check here first.

## Math

### [3Blue1Brown](/en/accounts/3blue1brown)

An English-language math channel that visualises abstract concepts with self-made animation — a globally recognised intuition primer (mainly English).

**Who it suits**: people who want to truly understand rather than memorise formulas — especially university students and self-learners.
**How to use it**: treat the animation as an intuitive teaching aid, with subtitles or a Chinese explainer to start; its value is in seeing math, not just computing it.

## History

### [Xiaoyuehan Kehan](/en/accounts/xiaoyuehan-kehan)

Known for series like "hardcore fierce people", telling historical figures and events like stories yet information-dense — an excellent on-ramp to general history.

**Who it suits**: people who find formal history dry and want a story-built historical thread.
**How to use it**: use it to build interest and a framework, then read stricter sources for depth.

### [Lishidiaoyanshi](/en/accounts/lishidiaoyanshi)

Deeper, evidence-based history with high information density — for those who want to dig.

**Who it suits**: readers unsatisfied with stories who want stricter synthesis.
**How to use it**: after a basic thread exists, use his depth as supplement and correction.

## How to judge whether a science account is trustworthy

Three workable tests:

**Does it explain logic and premises?** "A causes B" alone is worthless; explaining "under what premise, through what mechanism" is what has value.

**Does it separate opinion from fact?** A reliable account marks "this is the mainstream view" versus "this is my speculation", not packaging one opinion as settled.

**Does it cite sources?** Data, studies, and cases should trace to an origin. "They say" with no source deserves a question mark.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating fun as rigour.** Brain-teaser science is a good entry but not a substitute for systematic knowledge. Fun hooks interest; rigour lays the foundation.
- **Trusting a single account.** The same phenomenon is weighted differently across accounts; an official institute channel and a personal one complement each other best.
- **Ignoring premise conditions.** Many science conclusions have boundaries; applying them out of context easily yields wrong answers.
