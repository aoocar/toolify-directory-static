The biggest fear of college study planning is not "not working hard enough" — it is having **no timeline for four years**: a loose freshman and sophomore year, then realizing in junior year that foundations were never built and no direction was chosen. This guide gives a 5-step, executable route starting from day one.

All creators mentioned here have verified profiles on Dawn Island — click their names to see platforms, content focus, and verified data.

## Step 1: Build subject foundations (math + English)

The core task of the first semester is bridging the high-school-to-college gap:

- **Math**: Advanced mathematics is the first hurdle for most majors. [Song Hao](/en/accounts/songhao-laoshi)'s systematic courses are a widely recognized beginner-friendly entry point — worked examples, clear structure; [3Blue1Brown](/en/accounts/3blue1brown) visualizes the intuition behind math. Together they beat memorizing formulas.
- **English**: Take CET early. [Kaochong CET](/en/accounts/kaochong-siliuji) offers companion-style study plans and past-paper walkthroughs — ideal for building the pace in freshman year.

## Step 2: Pick your direction and major path

Don't settle in week one, but start building major awareness:

- [Zhang Xuefeng](/en/accounts/zhangxuefeng-laoshi) focuses on major choice, career planning, and grad-school advice, with plain-spoken opinions that help you think through "which major, which path".
- Combine your interests with employment reality and **settle a rough direction by sophomore year**; directions can adjust, foundations cannot be wasted.

## Step 3: Master study methods

Learning how to learn matters more than how much you learn:

- [Yi Shu](/en/accounts/yishu-laoshi) offers free systematic courses and study methods across high-school subjects — great for building foundations and a method system in freshman year.
- [oeasy](/en/accounts/oeasy) has complete, from-zero Office and computer tutorials — get your computer basics solid.

## Step 4: Plan the CET and grad-school pace

Put exam rhythm into your timeline:

- **CET**: take it in freshman or sophomore year; [Kaochong CET](/en/accounts/kaochong-siliuji) keeps the prep pace on track.
- **Grad-school math**: if you plan to pursue further study, start following [Zhang Yu](/en/accounts/zhangyu-kaoyan) (Basic 30 Lectures / Calculus 18 Lectures) in sophomore and junior year — systematic lectures plus problem training beat junior-year cramming.

## Step 5: Use free resources well

College is full of free high-quality resources — the key is using them:

- [Mofan Python](/en/accounts/mofan-python): programming basics from zero.
- [Li Mu](/en/accounts/li-mu-ai): learning route for deep learning and AI.
- [Peking University on Bilibili](/en/accounts/bidao-thu): open university lectures and hard-core knowledge.

## Four years in one sentence

**Freshman year: foundations. Sophomore: direction. Junior: exam prep. Senior: final push** — when you always know what you are preparing for, the four years won't be wasted.
