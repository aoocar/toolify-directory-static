College planning is the topic most likely to trigger anxiety during the back-to-school season: on one side, pressure to "win at the starting line"; on the other, information overload and nowhere to start. This guide does not sell anxiety — it groups creators **by college stage**, so you follow the ones that fit where you are right now.

All 7 creators mentioned are indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and verified data.

## Choosing a major & direction: the pragmatic planners

### [Zhang Xuefeng](/en/accounts/zhangxuefeng-laoshi)

One of China's best-known admission-planning educators and founder of Fengxue Weilai, covering gaokao volunteer filling, kaoyan school selection, major choice, and career prospects in a blunt, information-dense style.

**Who it suits**: students and parents planning gaokao, kaoyan, or a university major.
**How to use it**: before choosing a major or direction, listen to its analysis of how majors and schools compare and what career prospects look like — then weigh it against your own situation. It is practical experience, not academic advice.

## Learning methods & efficiency: the learning-science school

### [Labi & Xiaoxun](/en/accounts/labi-he-xiaoxun)

A duo turning learning science, cognitive psychology, and productivity research into practical, easy-to-apply study techniques.

**Who it suits**: freshmen who want to learn more effectively.
**How to use it**: treat it as a "study-method manual" — search it first when you hit memory, focus, or procrastination issues.

### [Yitiao Zhengxian](/en/accounts/yitiao-zhengxian)

A lifelong-learning creator sharing study habits and self-growth, guided by "growing steadily and moving forward in waves within the boundary of happiness."

**Who it suits**: freshmen who want sustainable study rhythms without burnout.
**How to use it**: use it as a reference for sustainable learning — how to balance effort and rest, rather than chasing "12-hour study days."

### [Peng Jiangjiang LINYA](/en/accounts/pengjiangjiang-linya)

A Cambridge-student creator sharing study vlogs, exam routines, and productivity habits.

**Who it suits**: students who want study role models and self-discipline methods.
**How to use it**: watch its study vlogs for a realistic reference of how top students study, then break its habits into small goals of your own.

## Exams & language: locking down the hard metrics

### [Kaochong CET-4/6 English](/en/accounts/kaochong-siliuji)

Kaochong Education's official Bilibili account, offering structured companion-style CET-4/6 preparation — vocabulary, listening, reading, and writing.

**Who it suits**: freshmen who want a steady CET-4/6 pass with a rhythm to follow.
**How to use it**: set your prep rhythm early, use its real-exam breakdowns to understand how the test works, then target your weak areas.

### [Junn Jiejun](/en/accounts/junn-jiejun)

An English & translation educator with 7 years of translation work and 12 years of teaching, offering systematic methods and language-growth paths.

**Who it suits**: freshmen who want genuine English improvement, not just exam cramming.
**How to use it**: treat it as a long-term English course — learn listening, speaking, reading, and writing as a system, not as vocabulary drill.

## Seeing how top students actually live: study abroad & academia

### [Cambridge LiaoLiao Xuejie](/en/accounts/jianqiao-liaoliao-xuejie)

A Cambridge University PhD sharing authentic doctoral study life, research rhythms, and academic-growth habits.

**Who it suits**: freshmen curious about study abroad, graduate school, and effective learning.
**How to use it**: use it as a realistic reference for top-university study rhythms, then figure out what you can borrow for yourself.

## Three tests: is it worth following long-term?

**One — does it clarify who it suits and the premises?** Advice that only shouts "this method will make you a legend" without saying who it suits and under what conditions is low-value.

**Two — are the methods verifiable?** Methods grounded in research, real experience, or reproducible data are more trustworthy; slogans and emotional hype deserve caution.

**Three — does it update consistently?** Learning and exam prep are long games; only channels that update systematically are worth following long-term.
