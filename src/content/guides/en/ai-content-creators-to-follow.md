AI is the fastest-changing content area of recent years — a new model almost monthly, yesterday's tutorial stale by morning. This guide does not rank by follower count. It groups creators by **what you want to do with AI**, to help you aim in the flood.

All ten creators below have profiles indexed on Dawn Island; click a name to see the platform, content focus, and any verified public figures.

## Want to build AI projects

### [Lin Yi LYi](/en/accounts/linyi-lyi)

A former algorithm engineer who on Bilibili uses AI for hardcore-yet-playful projects — from training an AI to beat fighting-game bosses to running an all-AI company, blending reinforcement learning, generative AI, and startup reality.

**Who it suits**: those who want to see AI actually "built", curious about both tech and entrepreneurship.
**How to use it**: as an idea bank and project sample; learn his engineering thinking of "turning an idea into a project".

## Want to study theory and papers

### [Tongji Zihao](/en/accounts/tongji-zihaoxiong)

Focused on AI paper reading, computer vision, large models, and robotics; his "AI paper reading" series and Stanford CS231N Chinese walkthrough are go-to resources for Chinese learners.

**Who it suits**: students and engineers who want to read papers and go deep into algorithms.
**How to use it**: as a systematic textbook; build a CV and LLM framework in his series order.

### [Gengzhi Ge](/en/accounts/gengzhige)

An AI PhD who explains large models, papers, and research trends in a straight, no-nonsense style, making the math and ideas behind modern AI accessible.

**Who it suits**: those who want to keep up with the frontier without being scared off by formulas.
**How to use it**: as a "frontier radar" for quick intuition on LLMs and research trends.

### [shuhuai008](/en/accounts/shuhuai008)

Known for the "machine learning whiteboard derivation" series, deriving ML algorithms from scratch step by step, rigorous and gradual.

**Who it suits**: learners who want true theory, not just package-calling.
**How to use it**: when you want to crack an algorithm's math, follow the whiteboard — more useful than reading conclusions.

## Want to use tools and get started

### [Qiuye aaaki](/en/accounts/qiuye-aaaki)

Widely regarded as the person who brought Stable Diffusion to Chinese creators; his one-click SD / ComfyUI / WebUI packs lowered the barrier for millions, and his model leaderboards remain a staple reference.

**Who it suits**: ordinary people who want AI art but were put off by environment setup.
**How to use it**: start with his intro and packs, then follow the model leaderboard to stay with the community mainstream.

### [GenJi](/en/accounts/genji-shizhen-ai)

A 2025 Bilibili Top-100 creator focused on AI application education — AI software development, animation, LLM principles, voice cloning; his "90 minutes to use AI" series brings advanced AI to millions.

**Who it suits**: workplace people who want a systematic, zero-to-usable path.
**How to use it**: as an "application course", following his series from tool to scenario step by step.

### [Lao Mai's Tool Library](/en/accounts/laomai-gongjuku)

Surfaces the newest and hottest AI tools — Sora, Claude, every model drop — with first-hand tests and practical efficiency workflows; among Bilibili's top AI creators.

**Who it suits**: those who do not want to miss new tools and prefer real tests before adopting.
**How to use it**: as a "tool intelligence station"; when a new model drops, watch his test first to skip your own pitfalls.

### [Qiuzhi 2046](/en/accounts/qiuzhi-2046)

A former product manager turned AI creator sharing tool reviews, hands-on teaching, and industry observation; directed Bilibili's first AI Spring Festival Gala in 2026.

**Who it suits**: ordinary users wanting both "how to use" and "where the industry goes".
**How to use it**: tool practice from her reviews, industry judgement from her observation — covering both ends.

## Want to patch coding and ML basics

### [Mofan Python](/en/accounts/mofan-python)

A long-time Bilibili coding educator whose short, punchy Python and ML tutorials accompanied a generation of Chinese beginners, covering data processing, sklearn, neural nets, and RL.

**Who it suits**: absolute beginners who want a solid code base.
**How to use it**: as an entry textbook; do Python and sklearn first, then papers and projects above.

## Want trends and the founder view

### [Zhou Hongyi (Red Coat Uncle)](/en/accounts/hongyi-dashu-zhouhongyi)

Founder of 360, viral as "Red Coat Uncle", posting almost daily on AI, cybersecurity, and startup management, and proposing an L1–L5 "agent evolution" framework.

**Who it suits**: those who want the entrepreneur and industry view of where AI goes.
**How to use it**: as a "trend wind vane" — but remember his content carries a stance endorsing his own AI products; listen broadly.

## How to judge whether an AI-teaching channel is trustworthy

Three workable tests:

**Does it explain principle, not just show effect?** "Watch me one-click generate" is fun, but a channel that cannot say why leaves you unable to adapt.

**Does it label timeliness and version?** AI tools iterate fast; tutorials without version or date may not run three months later.

**Does it separate "can demo" from "can deploy"?** Demo environments are clean, real ones messy; a reliable channel states the boundary.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing new models, skipping principle.** Hype changes quarterly; principle lasts years. Base beats fad.
- **Watching without practising.** AI is hands-on; running one project beats ten videos.
- **Swayed by "quick mastery".** "Master LLMs in three days" is mostly clickbait; systems engineering takes time.
